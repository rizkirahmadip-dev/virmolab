Methods & criteria

The scientific detail behind what VirmoLab does, for writing the Methods section of a manuscript.

Pocket detection

P2Rank 2.5.1 (default model, with a separate Java 21): solvent surface points are scored for ligandability by a random forest and then clustered into pockets; ranking follows the score, with a calibrated probability. Grid box: the pocket centre, with a size of the pocket residues' atom range plus 4 Å per axis, bounded to 16–30 Å.

Case-by-case differences remain (redocking MK1 into 1HSG missed by 9.9 Å with Uni-Dock and was correct at 1.7 Å with Vina), so a pose that is going to be reported is better checked again with Vina or AutoDock-GPU.

Covalent docking (tethered method)

The ligand is reacted chemically with the target residue's side chain (Cys, Ser, or Lys) in RDKit according to its reactive group: addition (Michael acceptors, activated alkynes, nitriles, aldehydes — the bond order drops by one and the nucleophile binds the electrophilic atom), substitution (α-haloketones and haloacetamides, sulfonyl fluorides — the leaving group is removed), or imine formation (aldehyde + Lys → a Schiff base). The residue–ligand adduct is built in 3D (ETKDGv3, MMFF94 minimisation), then its CA–CB axis is aligned to the residue's CA–CB position in the crystal structure. The adduct is converted by Meeko as a flexible residue rooted at CA (adapt_pdbqt_for_autodock4_flexres) and docked with AutoDock-GPU using --flexres alone, with no separate ligand, against a receptor whose target residue side chain has been split off.

The score AutoDock-GPU reports for such a pose is the flexible residue energy (adduct–receptor plus adduct intramolecular) without a torsional penalty: it is not a binding ΔG, so Ki and ligand efficiency are not shown and scores are compared only between covalent ligands on the same target residue. Quality control of a covalent pose uses PoseBusters without the minimum distance to protein test, since a covalent bond is by nature shorter than a van der Waals contact; the other 21 tests still run. Internal validation: 6OIM (KRAS G12C–sotorasib, Cys12), ligand built from SMILES, rank-1 pose at RMSD 1.34 Å against the crystal ligand.

Derived quantities

  • Estimated Ki = exp(ΔG / RT), with R = 1.987207 × 10⁻³ kcal mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ and T = 298.15 K.
  • Ligand efficiency (LE) = −ΔG / heavy atom count (kcal/mol per heavy atom).
  • Redocking RMSD: heavy atoms, without alignment (pose and crystal share the receptor's coordinate frame), taking the minimum over all molecular symmetry mappings; computed on a graph without bond orders so that protonation does not affect it. Valid at ≤ 2.0 Å.
  • Reference RMSD (AutoDock): the RMSD against the input ligand coordinates, read from the .dlg file.
  • Interaction fingerprint (IFP): the set of “residue × interaction type” bits (criteria above, van der Waals excluded) for the best pose; similarity is the Tanimoto coefficient |A∩B| / |A∪B|. The reference for the “IFP vs crystal” column is the IFP of the native ligand's crystal pose against the same receptor.
  • Per-atom energy contribution: the intermolecular terms of the AutoDock Vina scoring function (Trott & Olson 2010) recomputed per heavy atom pair — XS types from the AutoDock types, bonds from covalent distances; surface distance d = r − R₁ − R₂; gauss1 (−0.0356), gauss2 (−0.00516), repulsion (0.840), hydrophobic (−0.0351), hydrogen bond (−0.587); 8 Å cutoff — and assigned entirely to the ligand atoms. The sum comes close to the “Final Intermolecular Energy” of vina --score_only (1HSG/crystal indinavir: −19.28 against −19.27 kcal/mol).

Iterative screening (active learning)

Large libraries are screened following MolPAL (Graff, Shakhnovich & Coley, Chem Sci 2021;12:7866–7881): compounds are represented as 2048-bit hashed atom-pair fingerprints (topological distances of 1–3 bonds, the MolPAL default), a random initial batch is docked, then a surrogate model is retrained on every score obtained so far and an acquisition function picks the next batch. Models: an ensemble of three feed-forward neural networks (two hidden layers of 100 neurons) or a 100-tree random forest; uncertainty comes from the spread of predictions across members or trees. Acquisition: UCB (prediction − 2 × uncertainty), greedy, or random as a baseline. The model's prospective accuracy is measured on every batch before that batch is docked (Spearman ρ, Pearson r, MAE).

Retrospective test (Enamine docking scores from the MolPAL repository, thymidylate kinase 4UNN; an initial batch of 1 % plus 5 rounds × 1 % = 6 % of the library; the fraction of the best 1 % of compounds found, as mean ± standard deviation over repeats):

Neural network + UCB63.1 ± 4.4 %72.2 ± 0.7 %
Random forest + greedy52.9 ± 7.4 %56.7 ± 5.9 %

In other words: docking 6 % of a library finds roughly three quarters of its best 1 % of compounds — more than ten times better than picking at random, while still being no guarantee of finding every strong compound. The test script is dev/tolok_ukur_al.py.

Editing in the pocket (3D)

Analogues are built directly on the docked pose: one group is attached at a chosen atom (or a functional group is swapped for its bioisostere through a SMARTS reaction), the analogue's conformation is generated with the shared core (MCS) pinned to the parent pose's coordinates (RDKit ETKDGv3 with a coordinate map, then MMFF94 minimisation of the new group alone), and the pose is then minimised in place and scored with AutoDock Vina --local_only. Reported: the local ΔG, the ΔΔG against the parent pose, the ligand efficiency, the number of heavy atoms clashing with the protein (< 1.7 Å) before minimisation, and MW, logP, QED, and SAScore. Limits: a local score ranks suggestions against the parent pose, it is not an affinity; the binding mode is taken as fixed, so an analogue that would need a different pose is scored too low. A promising suggestion has to be redocked from scratch.

Glycosides and aglycones

Sugar recognition (adapted from the Sugar Removal Utility, Schaub et al., J Cheminform 2020;12:67): an isolated five- or six-membered ring (neither fused nor spiro) holding exactly one ring O, with every ring carbon sp3, and at least half of the ring carbons bearing an exocyclic O or N. Cutting: the single bond between the anomeric carbon (the ring carbon neighbouring the ring O) and an exocyclic O/N/S that is still bonded to another heavy atom is broken; that heteroatom stays on the aglycone and is given a hydrogen, as in enzymatic hydrolysis. An O bridging two anomeric carbons (sucrose) is cut once. An anomeric C–C bond to an aglycone ring (a C-glycoside) is kept. A sugar-ring fragment that is aromatic or larger than 2 × the ring size + 3 heavy atoms is not treated as a sugar (the glucose core of a hydrolysable tannin, for instance). An aglycone with fewer than 6 heavy atoms is flagged as not meaningful. The aglycone skeleton is matched against the project's ligands by canonical SMILES, or by the first 14 characters of the InChIKey when stereochemistry or tautomer differ.

Quantities, computed only for pairs docked with the same receptor, the same box (centre and size rounded to 0.1 Å), and the same engine; ΔG is that pair's best score under those conditions:

  • ΔΔG = ΔG of the glycoside − ΔG of the aglycone (negative means the glycoside scores more strongly).
  • LE = −ΔG / heavy atoms (Hopkins et al., Drug Discov Today 2004;9:430–431).
  • FQ = LE / LEscale, with LEscale = 0.0715 + 7.5328/HA + 25.7079/HA² − 361.4722/HA³ (Reynolds et al., Bioorg Med Chem Lett 2007;17:4258–4261). FQ removes the fall in LE that comes from size alone.
  • Contribution per sugar atom = −ΔΔG / (HA of the glycoside − HA of the aglycone). When this is smaller than the aglycone's LE, the glycoside's score advantage comes mainly from its size (“Ahead because of size”); when it is equal or larger, the sugar is probably making a specific interaction.

Aglycones are worth studying because dietary flavonoid O-glycosides are generally deglycosylated by small intestinal beta-glucosidase before absorption (Németh et al., Eur J Nutr 2003;42:29–42). Tests (dev/uji_glikosida.py): 26 PubChem reference molecules; the aglycone produced by cutting matches the PubChem aglycone entry for rutin, naringin, hesperidin, quercitrin, cosmosiin, arbutin, amygdalin, digitoxin, and adenosine; vitexin and mangiferin are not cut; sucrose is recognised as entirely sugar; pentagalloylglucose, topiramate, and ibuprofen are not glycosides.

Pose quality control (PoseBusters)

The best pose of every ligand is checked automatically after docking with PoseBusters 0.6.5 (the dock configuration; Buttenschoen et al. 2024), in a separate process so the interface stays responsive. Older projects are filled in automatically when opened. The PoseBusters column in the Results tab reads Pass when every test passes (“PB-valid”), or Fail (n) when n tests fail (hover for the detail). Statistics and charts

The Statistics tab (Analysis › Statistics and charts). One data point is one ligand in one job (its best pose). Choose a single job to compare only results from the same engine and parameters; “All jobs” uses each ligand's best ΔG.

  • Histogram: the number of bins chosen automatically (Freedman–Diaconis rule, 5–30 bins) or set by hand; the dashed line is the mean.
  • Scatter + regression: least-squares linear regression, R², Pearson's r and Spearman's ρ with two-sided p values (scipy.stats), and a band showing the 95 % confidence interval of the regression line.
  • Top ΔG bars: the N ligands with the lowest ΔG.
  • Correlation matrix, Pearson or Spearman (complete pairs per cell); * marks p < 0.05 without multiple-testing correction.
  • Estimated pKi = −ΔG / (RT ln 10).
  • Reading |r| (Evans 1996): < 0.2 very weak, 0.2–0.4 weak, 0.4–0.6 moderate, 0.6–0.8 strong, ≥ 0.8 very strong — guidance only.

A correlation shows a relationship, not causation. Export: plots as SVG/PDF (vector) or PNG/TIFF at a chosen DPI, data as CSV (for Excel, SPSS, R, or GraphPad Prism), and a “Statistics” sheet (descriptives per job) in the XLSX.

Consensus scoring

  • Score columns: each ligand's best ΔG per job (engine and parameters), and rescoring of the best poses with AutoDock Vina 1.2 --score_only --autobox using the Vina and Vinardo scoring functions (no pose optimisation).
  • Per column: average ranks for ties (1 = lowest ΔG) and an inverted z-score (higher is better). Only ligands with a value in every column are analysed.
  • The consensus order is the mean rank, with the mean z-score breaking ties. Consistent means a rank ≤ ⌈n × percentile⌉ in every column.
  • Agreement: Kendall's W with a tie correction, χ² = m(n − 1)W with df = n − 1 (equivalent to the Friedman test; verified identical against SciPy); Spearman's ρ between each pair of columns.
  • Vinardo reference: Quiroga R, Villarreal MA. Vinardo: a scoring function based on AutoDock Vina improves scoring, docking, and virtual screening. PLoS One. 2016;11(5):e0155183. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0155183

Statistical tests (the Tests & Plots tab)

  • Normality: Shapiro–Wilk per group (n ≥ 3). Homogeneity of variance: Brown–Forsythe (median-based Levene). α = 0.05.
  • Automatic selection — 2 groups: Welch's t-test when all groups are normal, otherwise Mann–Whitney (paired: paired t / Wilcoxon signed-rank). ≥ 3 groups: all normal and variances homogeneous → one-way ANOVA + Tukey HSD; normal but variances unequal → Welch ANOVA + Games–Howell; not normal → Kruskal–Wallis + Dunn (Holm correction). Manually, additionally: ANOVA with Dunnett against the first column.
  • Effect sizes: Cohen's d and Hedges' g (two groups), Cohen's dz (paired), r = z/√N (Mann–Whitney), η² (ANOVA), ε² (Kruskal–Wallis).
  • Dose–response: least-squares nonlinear regression of the four-parameter logistic model Y = Bottom + (Top − Bottom) / (1 + 10^((logIC₅₀ − X)·Hill)), with X = log₁₀ concentration; the 95 % CI of IC₅₀ comes from the standard error of logIC₅₀ (t distribution, df = n − 4) and is then back-transformed.
  • ROC: AUC = the Mann–Whitney statistic U/(n₁·n₀) (ties counted as ½); standard error and 95 % CI by the DeLong method (placement components); p from the two-sided Mann–Whitney test (H₀: AUC = 0.5). Threshold: score ≥ t means positive (or ≤ t for “low score = positive”, as with ΔG). The optimal cut-off maximises the Youden index (sensitivity + specificity − 1). Enrichment factor EFx% = (actives in the top x% / size of the top x%) / (total actives / total size).
  • Early enrichment: BEDROC (α = 20, 80 % of the weight on the top ±8 %; Truchon & Bayly 2007, matched identically against RDKit) and logAUC = the mean sensitivity on a log₁₀ FPR axis from 0.001 to 1, ×100 % (random gives 14.46 %; corrected logAUC = logAUC − 14.46; Mysinger & Shoichet 2010). Equal scores are ordered negatives first, which is the conservative choice. 95 % CIs for EF1%, EF5%, and BEDROC: stratified percentile bootstrap (1000 repeats, fixed seed). Plots: ROC, semi-log ROC, and an enrichment curve with ideal and random lines.
  • Contingency: a 2 × 2 table → two-sided Fisher's exact test (primary), χ² with Yates' correction, the odds ratio (95 % CI by Woolf) and relative risk (95 % CI by Katz) of the first row against the second, with the Haldane–Anscombe correction (+0.5) when a cell is zero. An r × c table → Pearson's χ²; when more than 20 % of cells have an expected frequency below 5, or any is below 1, an r × c Fisher exact test is used (SciPy). Effect size: Cramér's V. Verified: AUC = U/(n₁·n₀) exactly; Fisher's “tea tasting” example gives p = 0.4857 and OR = 9, as published.
  • Multiple-comparison correction: Holm, Bonferroni, Šidák (1 − (1 − p)m), Benjamini–Hochberg (FDR; identical to scipy.stats.false_discovery_control).
  • Repeated measures (≥ 3 paired columns): one-way within-subject ANOVA, with condition, subject, and error sums of squares; the Greenhouse–Geisser ε from the doubly centred covariance matrix, with p from F using df × ε (verified: F identical to an OLS subject + condition model). Nonparametric: Friedman (identical to SciPy) with Kendall's W as effect size; post-hoc paired t or Wilcoxon signed-rank plus the chosen correction.
  • Two-way ANOVA: an OLS model with effect coding (sum-to-zero), type III sums of squares obtained by comparing the full model against the model without that effect (valid for unbalanced data; identical to the classical formulas when balanced), η²ₚ = SS/(SS + SS error). Post-hoc: differences between A levels within each B level, t using the pooled error MS, 95 % CIs, with Šidák (the default) or another correction. Residuals: Shapiro–Wilk; between-cell variances: Brown–Forsythe.
  • Further normality: D'Agostino–Pearson K² (n ≥ 8). Q–Q plot: theoretical normal quantiles at Blom positions ((i − 0.375)/(n + 0.25)) against standardised values. Outliers: two-sided Grubbs G = max|x − x̄|/s, p = min(1; 2n·P(Tn−2 > t)) (G = 2.290 at n = 10 gives p = 0.05, matching the critical-value tables).
  • Dose–response model selection: 3PL is 4PL with the Hill slope fixed at ±1. Extra sum-of-squares F test F = (SS₃ − SS₄)/(SS₄/(n − 4)), df (1; n − 4); AICc = n·ln(SS/n) + 2K + 2K(K + 1)/(n − K − 1) with K = the number of parameters + 1; Akaike weights give the model probabilities. Automatic mode uses 4PL when p < 0.05.
  • Validation against R (the PlantGrowth dataset): ANOVA F(2; 27) = 4.846, p = 0.0159; Tukey p = 0.391 / 0.198 / 0.012; Kruskal–Wallis H = 7.988, p = 0.0184; Welch ANOVA F = 5.181, df₂ = 17.13, p = 0.0174 — identical.

Advanced analysis tools — formulas and their limits

This section sets out how the estimating tools in the Analysis menu compute what they report. State their limits whenever the numbers go into a manuscript.

The ligand route needs internet access (with a 7-day local cache). A target that fails to dock is not ranked.
Networks & pathway enrichment (F3b)Over-representation enrichment with a one-sided hypergeometric test P(X ≥ k) on Reactome (v97) or GO biological process (human GOA) gene sets, Benjamini–Hochberg corrected; the background is every annotated gene in the GMT file, reported as such. Target–target edges from STRING v12.5 (combined score ≥ a threshold of 0.4/0.7/0.9) and target–disease edges from the Open Targets Platform (association score ≥ a threshold, top five per target). Graph metrics: degree, betweenness, closeness (NetworkX). Sensitivity analysis: the graph is rebuilt for each combination of STRING threshold and number of top targets; the highest-degree target nodes appearing in ≥ 80 % of combinations are reported as stable core nodes.Warning: a STRING score includes co-expression and text-mining evidence, not only physical interaction; a network is a hypothesis and needs experimental validation. Every database is accessed online (7-day cache) with the version recorded in the data-source trail.
QSAR & prediction models (F5)pIC50 endpoints from unit conversion (p = −log₁₀ of the molar concentration); structures are standardised and duplicate measurements merged as a median. Features: Morgan2 (2048 bits), MACCS, or filtered RDKit 2D descriptors (zero variance and |r| > 0.95 removed). scikit-learn models (Random Forest, RBF SVR, kNN, Gaussian Process, Ridge/Logistic) with a grid search and 5-fold cross-validation inside the training set. Validation: CV Q², test-set R² and RMSE, the Golbraikh–Tropsha criteria, 50× Y-randomisation, and split conformal prediction at 90 % calibrated on a held-out part of the training set. Applicability domain: leverage h* = 3(p+1)/n when there are fewer features than compounds, plus Tanimoto similarity to the five nearest neighbours with the training set's 5th-percentile threshold. Interpretation: permutation importance on the test set.Warning: a random split gives over-optimistic metrics; the Bemis–Murcko scaffold split is reported beside it and a large gap is flagged by the application. Predictions for compounds outside the applicability domain must not be read as quantitative values. Reporting follows the five OECD principles for QSAR model validation.
Pharmacophore & 3D shape screening (F4)A 3D pharmacophore query is extracted from the reference pose using RDKit's BaseFeatures.fdef feature definitions (donor, acceptor, aromatic, hydrophobic); protein heavy atoms within 4.0 Å of the reference ligand become exclusion spheres of radius 1.2 Å. Candidate conformers are generated with ETKDGv3 (0.5 Å RMSD pruning), then each conformer is rigidly aligned with rdShapeAlign (shape and colour Tanimoto). The pharmacophore score is (the fraction of query features matched within tolerance) × (the distance precision 1 − RMSD/2t), minus 15 % per exclusion sphere penetrated; the final score is the weighted mean of the pharmacophore and shape-colour scores, taken from the best conformer. Query validation: ROC-AUC, BEDROC (α = 20), and EF 1 % over user-supplied actives and property-matched DUD-E-style decoys from the local library.Warning: a ligand-based method judges only similarity to one reference pose, not binding energy. An internal benchmark (a query from the 1HSG co-crystallised ligand; 8 approved HIV protease inhibitors against 520 approved drugs matched on molecular weight and rotatable bonds, Morgan2 Tanimoto < 0.35) gave a ROC-AUC of 0.66 and weak early enrichment — treat it as a coarse filter before docking. EF 1 % is not reported for sets below 100 compounds, because the top 1 % is then less than one compound.
Hypothesis tracker → laboratory assays (F12)Microplate readings are normalised against the controls in the plate layout: % activity = (S − S̄blank) / (S̄solvent − S̄blank) × 100, and % inhibition = 100 − % activity. All replicates of a compound are fitted to the four-parameter logistic model y = b + (a−b)/(1 + 10^((log IC₅₀ − log x)h)) (scipy.optimize.curve_fit), then pIC50 = −log₁₀ of the molar IC50. Plate quality is reported as Z′ = 1 − 3(σsolvent + σcontrol+)/|μsolvent − μcontrol+|. Agreement between prediction and measurement is measured with Spearman's ρ and Kendall's τ rank correlations with bootstrap 95 % confidence intervals (1000 repeats); the sign of energies (ΔG, MM/GBSA) is inverted so that a positive correlation means the rankings agree.Warning: an IC50 outside the concentration range tested is not extrapolated — it is marked censored (“IC50 > 100 µM”, say) and left out of the correlation. Z′ < 0.5 means the assay window is too narrow for a reliable IC50. A correlation with fewer than 8 pairs is flagged: its confidence interval is wide and the value unstable.
Boltz-2 co-folding and pose agreement (F6)Boltz-2 is run inside WSL for one receptor–ligand pair; the complex structure it returns is superposed onto the project receptor with the Kabsch algorithm on matching Cα atoms, and the ligand RMSD is then computed in that shared coordinate frame. Binding mode similarity is measured as the Tanimoto coefficient of the interaction fingerprints (IFP) between the docked and co-folded poses, and both poses are checked with PoseBusters. The agreement label follows written rules: Agree when the ligand RMSD is < 2.0 Å and both poses pass PoseBusters; Partial when the ligand RMSD is < 2.0 Å but a pose fails, or when the RMSD is larger yet the IFP Tanimoto is ≥ 0.6; otherwise Conflicting. An RMSD that cannot be computed yields an empty label rather than Conflicting. Affinity values and confidence metrics are read as they stand from the Boltz-2 output files.Warning: Boltz-2 was trained on PDB structures, so targets and ligands already in the PDB risk training-data leakage, and high agreement in such a case is not evidence of predictive ability. VirmoLab checks the ligand's similarity to ligands already in the PDB and warns accordingly. A Boltz-2 affinity is a model estimate, not a measurement. A Boltz or WSL failure is reported as an empty value, never replaced by a default number.
Peptide docking (F10)A peptide (≤ 30 residues) is not docked with a small-molecule scoring function but modelled as a separate protein chain in Boltz-2 co-folding alongside the receptor chain. The resulting complex is minimised with OpenMM in OBC2 implicit solvent (Amber ff14SB force field), with receptor heavy atoms harmonically restrained at 5 kcal/mol/Å² so that relaxation concentrates on the peptide; short dynamics is optional. The binding energy is estimated by single-trajectory MM/GBSA, treating the peptide chain as the binding partner. The interface is assessed by core/ppi.py: heavy atom contacts ≤ 4.5 Å, hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, and buried surface area (Shrake–Rupley ΔSASA).Warning: single-trajectory MM/GBSA is a rough estimate, useful for comparing peptides on the same target but not an absolute affinity prediction. The Boltz-2 training-data leakage warning applies in full to peptides whose complex is already in the PDB. The 30-residue limit is used because prediction becomes far slower and less trustworthy for longer chains. AutoDock CrankPep is not used because its licence status has not been verified as open source.
Semi-empirical electronic properties (F7)GFN2-xTB calculations (xtb 6.7.1pre) on a 3D conformer of the ligand, in ALPB implicit solvent or the gas phase. The frontier orbital energies give the gap ELUMO − EHOMO, chemical hardness η = (ELUMO − EHOMO)/2, chemical potential μ = (EHOMO + ELUMO)/2, and electrophilicity ω = μ²/2η. Condensed-charge Fukui indices (f+, f−, f0) are computed by xtb with --vfukui, partial charges use Mulliken analysis, and electrostatic potential points come from --esp; for colouring, ESP points are mapped to the nearest atom (averaged over eight points). Each molecule is computed in a separate process with a time limit.Warning: GFN2-xTB is a semi-empirical method, not DFT. Its HOMO–LUMO gap is systematically smaller than DFT's, so the numbers are only fit for comparing trends between ligands within one set of calculations, not as absolute values. An SCF that does not converge, or a calculation that exceeds the time limit, is reported empty rather than replaced by a default.
Docking-guided generative design (F8)Analogues are generated with CReM (chemically reasonable mutations): a fragment on the parent molecule is swapped for another fragment observed in the same chemical environment, with a context radius of 1 to 3 bonds. The fragment database is built from the local approved-drug library using cremdb_create. Duplicates are filtered by InChIKey. Filtering uses the RDKit PAINS and Brenk catalogues, SAScore, QED, and the Morgan fingerprint Tanimoto coefficient against the parent. The final ranking uses the first Pareto front over three objectives at once: docking binding free energy, QED, and SAScore.Warning: the local fragment database is far smaller than the full ChEMBL one, so the diversity of the analogues produced is narrower. The QED and SAScore thresholds follow the parent's values when the parent itself falls outside the standard thresholds; the thresholds actually used are recorded in the methods paragraph. An analogue is a computational suggestion whose synthetic feasibility has not been assessed; candidates that fail to dock are removed from the ranking and their number reported.
Alchemical relative free energy (F9, experimental)Perturbation pairs come from matched molecular pair analysis with a limit of 10 changed heavy atoms. Atom mapping uses the maximum common substructure (RDKit rdFMCS) matching element, bond order, and complete rings. The perturbation network is built as a star or a minimum spanning tree (Kruskal) over the number of changed atoms, plus cycle-closing edges. The free energy difference of each edge comes from the multistate Bennett acceptance ratio (MBAR, pymbar) over the reduced energies of every lambda window, and consistency is checked by cycle closure: the ΔΔG values around a closed cycle should sum to zero.Warning: the feature is labelled experimental. The alchemical simulations are NOT run by VirmoLab on Windows: OpenFE supports only Linux and macOS, so what is available here is network planning and the analysis of results computed elsewhere. A runtime estimate is shown only once the machine's speed has been measured, never guessed. RBFE is meaningful only for a congeneric series with small changes, and demands adequate sampling.

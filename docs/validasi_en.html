Validation: numbers you can check yourself

This page holds what VirmoLab measured on open reference datasets, together with how to run the measurements again. Every number here comes from real calculations on the developer's computer (RTX 3060 Laptop, Ryzen 7 6800H), not from someone else's paper, and the scripts that produced them ship with the source code.

How to read this page

  • RMSD ≤ 2 Å means the rank-1 pose lies at most 2 ångström from the crystal ligand (heavy atoms, ligand symmetry accounted for). This is the usual measure for calling a pose “correct”.
  • PB-valid means that pose also passes every PoseBusters physics check (geometry, clashes, internal energy). A pose with a small RMSD that fails the physics checks is not worth reporting.
  • 95 % confidence intervals use the Wilson method. With 85 to 428 cases, an interval of about ±10 % is to be expected: a difference of a few per cent between methods need not mean anything.
  • Comparison figures from papers are quoted as published, so you can judge for yourself whether the installation on your computer behaves sensibly.

1. Redocking: the Astex Diverse Set and the PoseBusters Benchmark Set

Two open datasets used by the PoseBusters paper (Buttenschoen et al., Chem Sci 2024): the Astex Diverse Set (85 selected crystal complexes) and the PoseBusters Benchmark Set (428 complexes released since 2021, chosen so as not to be training data for machine-learning methods). For each complex, VirmoLab prepares the receptor and the ligand with its own default workflow (gemmi, Meeko, PROPKA protonation, cofactors kept), uses a docking box that encloses the crystal ligand, then redocks the ligand with the Standard preset.

One PoseBusters case (7Q27, a boron-containing ligand) failed because AutoDock Vina does not know the B atom type; that is reported as a failure rather than counted as a score of zero. The PoseBusters set used here holds 428 complexes (the latest release), whereas the paper used the 308 complexes of the first version, so the figures are not strictly comparable — the 50.5 % here sits in the same range as the 60 % reported for the smaller and easier set.

Speed on Astex: a median of 11.9 seconds per ligand for Vina (CPU, exhaustiveness 16) and 3.5 seconds for Uni-Dock called one ligand at a time; in real use, where a batch holds tens to hundreds of ligands, Uni-Dock drops to roughly 0.7 seconds per ligand.

Case-by-case comparison over the same 85 complexes: 48 cases correct with both engines, 5 correct only with Vina, 1 only with Uni-Dock, the rest wrong with both. The two are therefore equivalent for screening, and a pose that is going to be reported is better checked again with Vina or AutoDock-GPU.

What follows from this: VirmoLab's default preparation workflow produces figures on a par with those reported for AutoDock Vina on the same datasets, and Uni-Dock sits slightly below Vina with overlapping confidence intervals. What does not follow: that your docking will be 60 % correct. Both datasets hold high-quality crystal complexes with pockets that are already formed; new targets, AlphaFold structures, and ligands outside crystallographic chemical space are harder.

2. Iterative screening (active learning)

Enamine docking scores from the MolPAL repository (thymidylate kinase 4UNN) stand in for docking, so a whole campaign can be simulated many times over. An initial batch of 1 % of the library, then 5 rounds × 1 % (6 % of the library docked in total); what is measured is the fraction of the best 1 % of compounds that were found.

StrategyEnamine 10k (5 replicates)Enamine 50k (3 replicates)
Neural network + greedy (default)63.7 ± 2.8 %74.4 ± 1.4 %
Neural network + UCB63.1 ± 4.4 %72.2 ± 0.7 %
Random forest + greedy52.9 ± 7.4 %56.7 ± 5.9 %
Random selection (reference)6.2 ± 2.5 %5.1 ± 0.1 %

So docking 6 % of a library finds roughly three quarters of its best compounds — more than ten times better than picking at random, while a quarter is still missed. The command: dev/tolok_ukur_al.py --set 50k.

For numbers meant to be reported, use OpenFE's own default protocol (5 ns × 3 repeats): on an RTX 3060 Laptop that means about 1.7 days per complex leg per edge. Test: dev/uji_rbfe_openfe.py.

4. Docking with flexible side chains, and the induced fit that was not released

Five cross-docking cases from Sherman et al. (J Med Chem 2006) — the crystal ligand of one structure docked into another structure of the same protein — were used to test whether a full induced-fit protocol (trim side chains, soft docking, OpenMM relaxation, redock) was worth releasing. It was not: 0 of 5 cases reached RMSD ≤ 2 Å, four of them were worse than rigid docking, while the scores appeared to improve (CDK2 −8.16 → −12.56 kcal/mol on the wrong pose). The feature was therefore not shipped; the details are in dev/dokumen/INDUCED_FIT_EKSPERIMEN.md.

What is used instead is ordinary flexible side chains (Vina --flex) with the six nearest pocket residues:

Case (receptor ← ligand)Rigid dockingFlexible, 6 residuesFull induced fit

Cross-docking really is hard: 1 of 5 cases correct with flexible side chains, 0 of 5 without. A sanity check: redocking each ligand into its own structure in the same cases gives 0.47 Å (1KIM) and 0.45 Å (1KI4), so the failures above are the difficulty of cross-docking, not an error in preparation.

5. Things validated elsewhere

  • Covalent docking: 6OIM (KRAS G12C–sotorasib, Cys12), rank-1 pose at RMSD 1.34 Å. See Methods & criteria.
  • Filling missing loops: a loop-deletion test on 3KS3 gives backbone RMSD of 0.20–0.56 Å for loops of 4–12 residues grafted from AlphaFold.
  • Pocket detection: the centre of the best pocket lies 0.9 Å from the MK1 ligand in apo 1HSG.
  • The protocol validation package inside the application (redocking, cross-docking, decoy enrichment, PoseBusters) produces numbers for your own protocol, and those are what belong in a manuscript, not the numbers on this page.

Limits worth remembering

  • A docking score is not a measured affinity. The correlation between score and activity is usually weak; what can be trusted comparatively is the shape of the pose and a coarse ranking.
  • The numbers on this page were measured on one computer with one version of each program. A different docking engine version, operating system, or GPU can shift them.
  • Reference datasets hold high-quality crystal complexes. Targets without a crystal structure, AlphaFold models, and pockets that only form on ligand binding are considerably harder than the figures above.
